## What changed

The Federal Trade Commission says global payment processor Nuvei will pay $4.85 million to settle charges that it opened and maintained payment-processing accounts for merchants it knew or should have known were engaging in deception. The FTC’s complaint included tech support scams that took millions of dollars from consumers. The allegations concern Nuvei and its subsidiaries; the settlement is not the same as a finding that every merchant using Nuvei was fraudulent. [S1]

The FTC’s announcement says Nuvei must implement robust merchant-screening practices, but the material provided here does not spell out the specific checks, monitoring standards or implementation timetable. It also does not describe a payment or claims process for consumers who lost money to a merchant in the case. [S1]

That distinction matters. The announced settlement targets a payment processor’s handling of merchant accounts. It does not, by itself, tell an individual consumer whether a particular charge is connected to the case or whether the consumer is eligible for money back. The FTC announcement supplied here does not identify affected consumers or promise individual refunds. [S1]

## What this means for consumers

If a charge or online purchase seems connected to a tech-support offer that misled you, treat the settlement as a reason to document and check the transaction—not as proof that your payment qualifies for reimbursement. The FTC describes the allegations as involving merchants that allegedly used deception, including tech support scams; it does not name specific merchants in the supplied announcement excerpt. [S1]

Start with the details you can verify: the date and amount, the merchant name shown on your statement, any order or service confirmation, and the messages or advertisements that led you to pay. Compare those records with the name of the business you believe you dealt with. A payment processor’s name, if it appears in your records, does not by itself establish which merchant sold the product or service or whether the transaction is part of this FTC matter. [S1]

If the business, charge or service cannot be reconciled, keep a clear timeline of what happened and save copies of the relevant records. Contact the business using contact information you can independently confirm and ask it to explain the charge and the service provided. Do not assume that a response—or silence—settles the question of whether the transaction relates to the FTC’s allegations. The announcement describes charges against Nuvei, not a finding about any specific consumer transaction. [S1]

You can also report suspected fraud to the FTC through its fraud-reporting channel. A report is a way to alert the agency; the available material does not say that reporting guarantees a refund, a response or a particular enforcement outcome. [S1][S2]

The practical decision path is straightforward: if the records clearly identify a legitimate purchase you recognize, keep them with your account records; if they show a service you did not receive or claims you believe were deceptive, preserve the evidence and seek an explanation; if you suspect fraud, consider submitting an FTC report. These steps help you organize what happened, but they do not establish eligibility for a payout under this settlement. [S1][S2]

## What to verify before you act

Before responding to a message about Nuvei, a refund or the FTC case, verify:

- **Who is contacting you.** The supplied FTC materials provide a fraud-reporting route, but do not identify a settlement administrator or describe an individual consumer payment program for this case. Do not treat an unsolicited message as proof that you are owed money. [S1][S2]
- **What the transaction was for.** Match the statement entry to your receipt, order confirmation, service agreement or other records. The FTC’s announcement describes allegations about merchant accounts and does not identify individual transactions. [S1]
- **What the FTC actually announced.** The reported amount is $4.85 million to settle FTC charges, and the announcement says Nuvei must implement merchant-screening practices. The supplied information does not set out consumer claim deadlines, eligibility criteria, payment dates or an individual refund process. [S1]
- **Where to report a concern.** Use the FTC’s own fraud-reporting channel rather than an unverified link or phone number in a message. The FTC lists reporting fraud as a consumer action. [S1][S2]

Keep the settlement in perspective: it addresses the FTC’s allegations about a processor’s relationships with merchants, while your next step depends on the evidence for your own transaction. Confirm the merchant, save the records and use an official reporting route if you believe you encountered fraud; do not rely on an unverified promise of payment. [S1][S2]
