A denial letter is not just a rejection. It normally identifies the policy language, factual reason or documentation issue the insurer relied on. That makes the denial letter the starting point for any review.

## Match the denial to the policy

Read the cited policy provision in context. Compare definitions, exclusions, endorsements and any conditions that apply to the claim.

## Separate missing evidence from a coverage dispute

A claim can fail because evidence is missing, because the insurer interprets the facts differently or because the insurer says the loss is outside coverage. Each problem requires a different response.

## Build a clean chronology

Create a timeline of the event, notice, inspections, estimates, communications and insurer decisions. Preserve originals where possible.

## Check the official appeal or complaint process

Appeal rights and complaint procedures vary. Use the current instructions from the insurer and the relevant insurance regulator rather than assuming one process applies everywhere.
