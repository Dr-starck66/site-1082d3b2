The Federal Trade Commission says payment processor Nuvei will pay $4.85 million to settle charges that it facilitated merchant fraud. The agency alleges Nuvei opened and maintained payment-processing accounts for merchants it knew or should have known were deceiving consumers, including tech-support scammers who took millions of dollars. [S1]

The announcement is about Nuvei’s alleged role in providing payment processing to merchants—not a finding in the supplied account that any particular reader’s transaction went through Nuvei. For consumers who paid a business they now suspect was deceptive, the practical question is still how to identify the seller and the payment route, then decide what to check and where to report concerns. [S1][S2]

## What changed

Nuvei and its subsidiaries are settling FTC charges for $4.85 million, and the FTC’s announcement says the company must implement robust merchant-screening practices. The agency’s complaint alleges that Nuvei opened and maintained accounts for merchants engaged in deception, including tech-support scams. These are allegations being settled; the supplied announcement does not provide the settlement’s detailed screening requirements or describe a consumer payment program. [S1]

The FTC says the alleged scams took millions of dollars from consumers. That figure describes the scale of the alleged consumer losses in the agency’s account; it is not a stated amount owed to any individual, nor does the supplied announcement say how the $4.85 million settlement payment will be used. [S1]

That distinction matters when deciding what to do next. The announcement concerns the processor’s alleged business practices and a required change to its screening. It does not identify individual affected customers, name all of the merchants at issue in the supplied text, or tell consumers to submit a claim. [S1]

## What this means for consumers

If a charge seems connected to a tech-support offer or another business you believe misled you, start with the transaction record: note the date, amount, and the merchant name shown by your bank, card issuer, or payment account. Then compare that name with the company you thought you were paying. This is a way to organize your own inquiry, not evidence that Nuvei processed the payment. The FTC announcement does not provide a lookup tool for tracing an individual transaction to Nuvei. [S1]

If you recognize the merchant but believe its claims or service were deceptive, keep the issue focused on what happened in your transaction: what was offered, what you paid, and what led you to believe the offer was misleading. The FTC’s consumer-advice site provides a route to report fraud and includes an article titled “What To Do if You Were Scammed.” The supplied material does not specify a filing deadline, guarantee a recovery, or say that reporting will result in a refund. [S2]

If you do not recognize the merchant name, don’t treat the Nuvei settlement as confirmation that the charge is connected. The FTC release describes allegations about accounts used by merchants, but the available text does not list merchants or provide a method to match consumer charges to those accounts. Check the name and details shown in your own payment records before drawing a connection. [S1]

For a suspected scam, use the FTC’s official consumer-advice site to find its fraud-reporting option rather than relying on a message or link claiming to represent the agency. The site extract lists “Report fraud” as a consumer action. It does not give further instructions in the supplied evidence, so verify the current reporting steps on the FTC site itself. [S2]

## What to verify before you act

Before you report or pursue a payment issue, check:

- **The transaction:** Is the charge present in your account, and what merchant name and amount appear? The settlement announcement does not identify individual charges. [S1]
- **The connection:** Do you have evidence linking the merchant or transaction to Nuvei? The supplied announcement does not include a consumer-facing transaction search or a complete list of the businesses involved. [S1]
- **The claim:** Are you reporting a specific experience—such as a tech-support interaction you believe was deceptive—or assuming the settlement covers you? The FTC describes alleged tech-support scams but does not identify individual consumers or announce a claims process in the supplied text. [S1]
- **The next step:** Are you using the FTC’s consumer-advice website to locate its fraud-reporting option? The site lists that option, but the provided material does not establish a deadline or promise a payment. [S2]

Keep the settlement and your own case separate: Nuvei’s $4.85 million payment is part of the FTC settlement, while the supplied announcement does not say that consumers will receive money from it. If you believe you were deceived, document the transaction and use the FTC’s listed reporting resource to check what to do next; do not rely on an unverified promise of compensation. [S1][S2]
