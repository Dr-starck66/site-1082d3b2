## What changed

The Federal Trade Commission says payment processor Nuvei and its subsidiaries will pay $4.85 million to settle charges that they opened and maintained payment-processing accounts for merchants they knew or should have known were engaging in deception. The FTC says the alleged conduct included tech-support scams that took millions of dollars from consumers. The agency’s announcement describes allegations and a settlement, not a consumer refund announcement. [S1]

The FTC announcement says Nuvei must implement robust merchant-screening practices. The supplied notice does not spell out the screening requirements, say when they take effect, or describe how the settlement money will be used. Consumers therefore should not treat the $4.85 million figure as an amount set aside for individual claims or as a promise of repayment. [S1]

That distinction matters if a charge on your account appears connected to a suspicious merchant. The announcement concerns the processor’s alleged role in serving merchants; it does not say that every transaction handled by Nuvei was fraudulent, identify a list of affected consumers, or provide a process for checking whether a particular payment is part of the case. [S1]

## What this means for consumers

Start with the transaction, not the settlement headline. If you see an unfamiliar payment, write down the amount, date, and merchant name shown on your statement, then compare it with receipts, emails, and any purchase records you have. The FTC notice does not provide a list of merchants or transactions tied to the allegations, so a match to the company name alone cannot establish that your payment is covered. [S1]

If the charge relates to a tech-support offer or another service you believe was deceptive, keep the records and contact the company or financial institution that handled your payment to ask what information they need to review it. This is a practical next step, not a promise that a reversal, refund, or other outcome is available. The FTC announcement does not set out an individual payment-dispute procedure. [S1]

If you are concerned that the incident involved your personal information or may affect your credit file, check your credit reports for information you do not recognize. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau recommends checking reports at least once a year and says requesting them does not hurt your credit score. It also points consumers to resources for reviewing reports and disputing errors. [S2]

A payment problem and a debt-collection notice are not the same thing. If someone contacts you to collect a debt you do not recognize, the CFPB says to use the contact to find out about the debt and determine whether you owe it. A collector is required to provide the creditor’s name, the amount owed, and information on how to dispute the debt; if the caller will not or cannot provide that information, the CFPB says it could be a scam. [S3]

Use this decision path:

- **Unrecognized payment:** Preserve the statement entry and related records, then ask the payment provider what review or dispute options apply. Do not assume the FTC settlement automatically covers your transaction. [S1]
- **Credit-report concern:** Review your reports for unfamiliar information and follow the CFPB’s guidance for disputing an error if you find one. [S2]
- **Debt collector contact:** Ask for the creditor, amount, and dispute instructions before deciding whether the debt is yours. If you believe you do not owe it, the CFPB says you should tell the collector. [S3]

These steps address different questions: whether a transaction is recognizable, whether a report contains an error, and whether a claimed debt is yours. Keeping them separate can help you direct each issue to the relevant company or process instead of relying on a general enforcement announcement to resolve a personal account problem. [S1][S2][S3]

## What to verify before you act

- **Check the claim:** The FTC announcement describes charges and a settlement; it does not say that every Nuvei-processed payment involved deception. Compare your own records before drawing a conclusion. [S1]
- **Check the terms:** The supplied announcement does not identify individual recipients, a claim form, a filing deadline, or the settlement’s distribution terms. Verify any claim instructions through an official FTC source rather than assuming one exists. [S1]
- **Check the record:** For a credit-report issue, use the CFPB’s credit-report and dispute resources. For a debt-collection issue, request the identifying information and dispute instructions the CFPB says a collector must provide. [S2][S3]

The settlement is a reason to pay attention to suspicious merchant activity, but it does not by itself answer whether a specific consumer lost money or what remedy applies. Keep transaction and contact records, verify the details through the appropriate channel, and avoid paying or sharing sensitive information in response to an unverified message claiming to secure a settlement refund. The supplied FTC notice does not describe a consumer refund process. [S1]
