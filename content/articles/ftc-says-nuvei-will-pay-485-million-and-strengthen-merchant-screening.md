The Federal Trade Commission says payment processor Nuvei will pay $4.85 million and strengthen its merchant screening practices to settle charges that it facilitated merchant fraud. The FTC alleged that Nuvei and subsidiaries processed payments for merchants they knew or should have known were engaged in deception, including tech support scams that took millions of dollars from consumers. [S1]

For consumers, the announcement is a reason to check the details of a suspicious charge—not a promise of a payment or a finding that every transaction handled by Nuvei was fraudulent. The FTC release excerpt describes the settlement and screening requirement but does not provide an individual claim process or say that consumers will receive money from the settlement. [S1]

## What changed

In a September 4, 2026, announcement, the FTC said Nuvei, a global payment processor, would pay $4.85 million to settle the agency’s charges. The complaint alleged that Canada-based Nuvei Corporation and its subsidiaries opened and maintained payment-processing accounts for merchants involved in deceptive activity. These are allegations described by the agency; the announcement says they were settled, not that every merchant or transaction connected to the processor was found fraudulent. [S1]

The settlement also requires Nuvei to implement robust merchant screening practices, according to the FTC’s announcement. The supplied release excerpt does not spell out the screening procedures or provide further detail about how they will be monitored, so consumers should not assume that the announcement guarantees a particular result for a past purchase or a future transaction. [S1]

The FTC highlighted tech support scams among the alleged merchant activity, saying those scams took millions of dollars from consumers. That gives people who paid for unexpected tech support a reason to review their payment records, but the release does not identify particular merchants, customers, or transactions that qualify for help. [S1]

## What this means for consumers

If a charge looks unfamiliar, first compare its date and amount with receipts, email confirmations, and subscriptions. A payment processor may appear in a transaction trail even when the consumer dealt with a separate seller, so an unfamiliar processor name alone does not establish who sold the product or whether the charge was fraudulent. The FTC describes Nuvei as a processor that handled payments for merchants. [S1]

If you identify a seller or payment you believe is deceptive, save the statement entry, receipt, order confirmation, and any messages or support records. Contact your bank, card issuer, or payment service using contact details from its official app or website, and explain what happened. Ask what steps are available for that specific transaction and whether any documentation is needed; the FTC announcement does not set out a consumer dispute deadline or guarantee a refund. [S1]

If the experience has you worried about identity theft or inaccurate credit information, check your credit reports. The CFPB recommends checking them at least once a year and says reviewing your reports does not hurt your credit score. If you find an error, the CFPB provides a guide to disputing credit-report errors. [S2]

If a debt collector contacts you about a balance connected to an unfamiliar purchase, do not assume that the Nuvei settlement resolves the debt. The CFPB says a collector is required to provide information including the creditor’s name, the amount owed, and how to dispute the debt; use that information to determine whether the account is yours and what your options are. [S3]

A practical decision path is: identify the seller behind the charge; gather the records; contact the payment provider about that transaction; then check your credit reports or request debt details if the issue appears on a report or leads to collection activity. The CFPB also lists ways to submit complaints and consumer guidance on fraud and debt collection. [S2][S3]

## What to verify before you act

- **The transaction:** Does the amount and date match a purchase you made, and can you identify the seller rather than only the processor name?
- **The contact channel:** Are you contacting the bank, card issuer, or payment service through an official app, website, or statement—not through a number or link in an unexpected message?
- **The next step:** What does your payment provider ask you to submit, and what deadlines or options apply to your particular transaction? The FTC release does not specify those details. [S1]
- **Credit or collection records:** If a report shows an unfamiliar account, consult the CFPB’s dispute guidance; if a collector contacts you, review the debt information and how to dispute it. [S2][S3]
- **Any settlement notice:** Do not pay someone or share sensitive information based only on a claim that they can obtain Nuvei settlement money for you. The supplied FTC release excerpt does not describe a consumer claims process. [S1]
