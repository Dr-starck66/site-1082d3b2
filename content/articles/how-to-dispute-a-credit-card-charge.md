A payment dispute is strongest when it is treated as an evidence problem rather than a complaint. Start by identifying exactly what happened, what you expected to receive, what the merchant promised and what documentation you can produce.

## Build an evidence file first

Save the receipt, order confirmation, merchant correspondence, delivery tracking, cancellation request and screenshots of the terms that applied when you bought. Keep dates and amounts exact.

## Contact the merchant when appropriate

Many disputes are resolved faster when the merchant has a clear opportunity to correct the problem. Keep the conversation factual and preserve a written record.

## Use the card issuer's formal dispute channel

The correct procedure and deadlines depend on the transaction, account agreement and applicable law. Follow the issuer's current instructions and do not rely on a generic deadline copied from another case.

## Keep a timeline

Write down every contact, reference number and response. If a dispute is denied, this timeline can make an escalation easier to understand.

## Verify current rules

Consumer-protection rules can change and specific protections differ by transaction type. Before acting, verify the current guidance published by the relevant regulator and your card issuer.
