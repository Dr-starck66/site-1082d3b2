The Federal Trade Commission says payment processor Nuvei will pay $4.85 million and implement stronger merchant screening practices to settle charges that it facilitated merchant fraud. The agency alleges Nuvei opened and maintained accounts for merchants it knew or should have known were engaged in deception, including tech-support scams that took millions of dollars from consumers. [S1]

The announcement is about the company’s alleged role in processing payments for merchants—not a notice that consumers are owed money. The supplied FTC announcement does not explain whether any part of the $4.85 million will go to customers, identify eligible consumers, or set out a refund process. [S1]

## What changed

On September 4, 2026, the FTC announced the settlement with Nuvei Corporation and subsidiaries. The agency’s headline says Nuvei must implement robust merchant screening practices, and the announcement says the firm will pay $4.85 million to settle the FTC’s charges. [S1]

The FTC alleges that Nuvei opened and maintained payment-processing accounts for merchants it knew or should have known were engaged in deception. The agency specifically points to tech-support scams that took millions of dollars from consumers; the supplied announcement does not provide details about particular merchants, affected customers, or individual transactions. [S1]

That distinction matters: these are charges the company is settling, not a basis in the supplied material for treating every allegation as a court finding about a particular purchase. The announcement describes the settlement and the FTC’s allegations, but the extract does not state that Nuvei admitted wrongdoing. [S1]

A payment processor can be part of a transaction without being the name a customer recognizes from a purchase. Still, the announcement does not say that Nuvei handled every payment for any named merchant, nor does it offer a way to identify a transaction processed by the company. Consumers should avoid assuming that an unfamiliar statement entry is connected to this case based on the announcement alone. [S1]

## What this means for consumers

For consumers, the practical takeaway is to separate three questions: Do you recognize the charge? Do you have a concern about the seller or service? And is there evidence connecting that payment to Nuvei or a merchant covered by the FTC matter? The announcement answers none of those questions for an individual transaction. [S1]

Start with the records you already have. Compare the amount and date on a statement with receipts, order confirmations, subscriptions, and any service paperwork. Note the merchant name shown on the statement, since the FTC announcement does not provide a list of merchant names consumers can use to match purchases to this case. [S1]

If the charge is unfamiliar, or the seller’s claims now seem deceptive, keep the relevant records together: the statement entry, receipt, messages, website details you saved, and a short timeline of what happened. This is a practical way to describe the issue accurately if you decide to seek help or make a report; it does not establish that your payment is part of the FTC case.

Do not treat the $4.85 million figure as a promised consumer payout. The source describes it as Nuvei’s payment to settle the FTC’s charges and does not announce a consumer refund program, an application deadline, or eligibility criteria. Anyone claiming that the FTC has already approved a payment for you based only on this announcement is going beyond the information provided. [S1]

If you believe you encountered fraud, the FTC’s consumer site provides a “Report Fraud” option. Make a report based on what you can document, and distinguish what you know firsthand from what you suspect about the merchant or payment processor. The supplied consumer-site extract lists the reporting option but does not state that reporting guarantees a refund or other outcome. [S2]

## What to verify before you act

Use this decision path before sharing information or responding to a message about the settlement:

- **A charge looks familiar:** Check your own purchase records first. The FTC announcement does not identify individual transactions or confirm that any particular charge involved Nuvei. [S1]
- **A charge or seller seems suspicious:** Gather the transaction details and communications you have. If you choose to report the concern, use the FTC consumer site’s fraud-reporting option rather than relying on an unsolicited message claiming to represent the agency. [S2]
- **Someone says you qualify for settlement money:** Verify the claim against an official FTC announcement. The supplied announcement gives no consumer payout instructions, deadline, or eligibility rules. Do not assume a payment is available—or provide sensitive information—on the strength of the $4.85 million figure alone. [S1]
- **You want to know whether your purchase is connected:** Look for a specific merchant or transaction link in reliable records. The supplied extracts do not name affected merchants or provide a lookup tool, so a connection cannot be established from the settlement headline alone. [S1]

The case puts merchant screening at the center of the FTC’s action against a payment processor. For a consumer deciding what to do next, the reliable starting point is narrower: verify the transaction, preserve the evidence, and report a suspected fraud through an official channel. The announcement signals an enforcement response; it does not, by itself, resolve an individual customer’s dispute. [S1][S2]
