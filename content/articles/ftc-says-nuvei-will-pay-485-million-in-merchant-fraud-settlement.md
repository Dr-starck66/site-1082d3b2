The Federal Trade Commission says payment processor Nuvei will pay $4.85 million to settle charges that it facilitated merchant fraud. The agency alleges Nuvei and its subsidiaries opened and maintained payment-processing accounts for merchants they knew or should have known were deceiving consumers, including through tech-support scams that took millions of dollars. [S1]

For consumers, the announcement is a reason to check the facts behind a suspicious charge or collection contact—not evidence that every Nuvei transaction was fraudulent or that a payment is automatically on its way back. The FTC’s announcement describes the settlement and screening requirements, but the supplied information does not identify a consumer refund program or say who, if anyone, may receive money. [S1]

## What changed

The FTC announced the settlement on September 4, 2026. Under the agreement described in the agency’s announcement, Nuvei must implement robust merchant-screening practices and pay $4.85 million to settle the FTC’s charges. The agency says the case concerns accounts maintained for merchants engaged in deception, including tech-support scams. [S1]

The central allegation is about what Nuvei knew or should have known when it opened and maintained merchant accounts. That is different from the FTC saying that every business using Nuvei was fraudulent, or that every consumer who paid one of those businesses has a claim under the settlement. The available announcement excerpt does not list the merchants, describe how consumers can check whether a particular purchase is connected to the case, or provide a claims process. [S1]

The headline promises stronger screening, but the supplied details do not spell out the screening steps, when they must take effect, or how compliance will be monitored. Those specifics matter for understanding what changes in practice; consumers should look to the FTC’s complete case materials for them rather than assume the headline describes every requirement. [S1]

## What this means for consumers

If a charge looks unfamiliar, first identify the merchant named on the transaction record. The FTC’s announcement links Nuvei to merchant processing, but the supplied information does not say that Nuvei itself sold goods or services directly to consumers. A processor’s involvement alone does not establish that a specific transaction was deceptive or that it is part of this case. [S1]

Keep the receipt, order confirmation, emails, texts, and any description of the service you were offered. Compare those records with the charge and note what you were promised and what, if anything, you received. This gives you a clearer basis to explain a concern if you contact the merchant or another organization about the transaction. The FTC announcement does not provide a case-specific process for resolving individual purchases. [S1]

If you believe a scam has affected your finances, check whether the issue also appears in your credit information. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau recommends checking credit reports at least once a year for errors that could lower a score or affect access to credit or loan terms. It also says requesting your credit reports will not hurt your score and provides guidance on disputing errors. [S2]

A collection call is a separate issue from a suspicious payment. The CFPB says a debt collector should provide the creditor’s name, the amount owed, and information on how to dispute the debt. If the caller will not or cannot provide that information, the CFPB says it could be a scam; if you believe you do not owe the debt, its guidance says to tell the collector. [S3]

Do not treat the $4.85 million figure as a payment estimate for an individual. It is the amount Nuvei will pay to settle the FTC charges, and the supplied announcement does not say that consumers will receive a share, how anyone could qualify, or when payments might be made. [S1]

## What to verify before you act

Use this decision path before sharing information, paying a collector, or relying on a claim that you are owed money:

- **Unrecognized charge?** Check the transaction record and your purchase documents to identify the merchant and what you agreed to buy. The FTC announcement does not identify affected consumers or provide a way to match an individual charge to the case. [S1]
- **Claim of a settlement payment?** Verify the claim against official FTC case information. The provided announcement names a settlement amount but does not describe consumer eligibility or a claims process. Do not assume a message offering money is legitimate based on the Nuvei case alone. [S1]
- **Credit-report concern?** Review your reports for information you believe is wrong, then use the CFPB’s guidance on disputing an error. Requesting a report does not hurt your credit score, according to the CFPB. [S2]
- **Debt collector contact?** Ask for the creditor’s name, amount, and how to dispute the debt. If you believe the debt is not yours, say so; the CFPB provides additional guidance on responding to collectors and disputing debts. [S3]
- **Still uncertain?** Check the full FTC case materials for settlement terms beyond the announcement excerpt. For credit-report issues, use CFPB resources; for collection questions, consult its debt-collection guidance. These sources explain general consumer steps but do not establish that a particular transaction or debt is covered by the Nuvei settlement. [S1][S2][S3]
