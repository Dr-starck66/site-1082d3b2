## What changed

The Federal Trade Commission announced a settlement with global payment processor Nuvei and several subsidiaries over allegations that they opened and maintained payment-processing accounts for merchants the companies knew or should have known were engaging in deception. The FTC said the alleged activity included tech support scams that took millions of dollars from consumers. Nuvei will pay $4.85 million and must implement robust merchant screening practices under the settlement. [S1]

The distinction matters: the FTC’s announcement describes charges and a settlement, not a finding here that every Nuvei merchant was fraudulent or that every transaction processed by the company involved wrongdoing. The allegations concern the firm’s relationship with merchants; the announcement does not identify affected consumers or provide a process for individual claims. [S1]

For someone who saw Nuvei on a bank or card statement, the processor’s name alone therefore does not establish whether a charge is legitimate. The practical question is what transaction the entry relates to: a purchase, subscription, service, or payment you do not recognize. The FTC announcement does not say that consumers will receive money from the $4.85 million settlement, explain who might qualify for any payment, or give a claim deadline. [S1]

## What this means for consumers

If you are checking a charge, start by matching its date and amount against receipts, order confirmations, subscriptions, and accounts used by other people on the payment method. Search your email for the amount or merchant name, but treat unexpected links or messages asking you to “verify” payment details cautiously. Nuvei is a payment processor, and the FTC’s allegations were about processing accounts for merchants; a processor name may not be the name you recognize from a purchase. [S1]

If you still cannot identify the transaction, contact the merchant using contact details you find independently, rather than relying on a suspicious message or unfamiliar link. Ask what was purchased, when, and under what account or order reference. Keep a record of the statement entry and any replies. If the merchant cannot explain it—or you believe the charge is unauthorized—contact the bank, card issuer, or payment service that handled your payment and ask what dispute process and deadlines apply to your specific transaction. The FTC announcement does not set those deadlines or promise a particular outcome. [S1]

For a suspected tech support scam, write down what happened while details are fresh: the company name used, the claim made, how you were contacted, what you paid, and whether you shared information or gave anyone access to a device. Preserve receipts and messages. These details can help you explain the situation when contacting your payment provider or reporting the incident. The FTC’s consumer site provides a way to report fraud and lists an article on what to do if you were scammed. [S2]

Do not assume that a settlement announcement means you need to pay a fee, share sensitive information, or act through someone who contacts you unexpectedly. The supplied FTC announcement does not describe a consumer refund program, a sign-up process, or a deadline related to this Nuvei settlement. Check the FTC’s own website before responding to anyone claiming they can get you settlement money. [S1][S2]

The useful decision path is: recognize the transaction if you can; ask the merchant for an explanation if you cannot; then contact the payment provider if the charge remains unexplained or unauthorized. Separately, report suspected fraud through the FTC’s consumer site. Keep the steps distinct: a report gives the FTC information, while questions about a particular charge belong with the merchant and the provider that handled your payment. The supplied sources do not say that filing a report will reverse a charge or produce a refund. [S1][S2]

## What to verify before you act

Before sharing details or following instructions, check:

- **The transaction:** Does the amount, date, and merchant name match a purchase, subscription, or account you recognize? Is another authorized user able to identify it?
- **The contact:** Did you find the merchant’s contact information independently, or did it come from an unexpected call, email, text, or pop-up?
- **The next step:** If you may dispute a charge, ask your bank, card issuer, or payment service what process and timing apply. Do not rely on the Nuvei settlement announcement for transaction-specific instructions; it provides no dispute deadline. [S1]
- **Any claimed settlement payment:** Is the information on an official FTC page? The announcement supplied here describes a payment by Nuvei to settle FTC charges, not a consumer application or individual payout. [S1]
- **The record:** Have you saved the statement entry, receipts, messages, merchant responses, and a short timeline of events?

If the charge is still unexplained, pursue the payment provider’s process and report suspected fraud through the FTC’s consumer site. Keep copies of what you submit and note any instructions or deadlines the provider gives you. Neither the settlement nor a fraud report guarantees that a particular charge will be refunded. [S1][S2]
