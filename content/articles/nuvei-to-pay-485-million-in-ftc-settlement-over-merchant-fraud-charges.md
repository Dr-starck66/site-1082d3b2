## What changed

Payment processor Nuvei will pay $4.85 million to settle Federal Trade Commission charges that it opened and maintained payment-processing accounts for merchants it knew or should have known were engaging in deception. The FTC’s September 4 announcement says the alleged conduct included tech-support scams that took millions of dollars from consumers. The agency’s complaint names Canada-based Nuvei Corporation and its subsidiaries. [S1]

The settlement also requires Nuvei to implement robust merchant-screening practices, according to the FTC’s headline. That matters because a payment processor can sit between a customer’s payment and a merchant without being the business a customer intended to buy from. The FTC’s allegation is that Nuvei’s role went beyond simply handling payments: it allegedly continued to provide accounts to merchants despite warning signs of deception. [S1]

The distinction between an allegation and a finding is important. The FTC describes these as charges in a complaint and says Nuvei will pay to settle them; the material supplied here does not give the settlement’s detailed screening requirements, explain whether Nuvei admitted wrongdoing, or identify a consumer refund program. [S1]

## What this means for consumers

For a person who paid a fraudulent merchant, the settlement is not, by itself, a notice that a refund is coming. The announcement provided here does not name eligible consumers, describe a claims process, set a filing deadline, or say that the $4.85 million will be distributed to people who lost money. Don’t assume you qualify for payment—or that you need to pay someone to claim it—based on the settlement headline alone. [S1]

If a charge looks unfamiliar or seems connected to a deceptive service, start with the transaction itself. Check the date, amount, and merchant name on your account against receipts, emails, and any service you recently bought. A payment statement may show a name you don’t recognize, so treat that as a reason to investigate rather than proof on its own that the charge is fraudulent. This is a practical first check, not a guarantee of a particular outcome.

If you think you were scammed, use the FTC’s consumer site to report fraud and find its consumer advice. Keep copies of the charge details and communications so you can explain what happened when seeking help. Reporting a suspected scam does not establish that a payment will be reversed or that you are eligible for money from this settlement. [S1][S2]

Next, contact the business shown on the transaction only if you can independently confirm it is the legitimate merchant, and contact your bank or card issuer using the number on your card or its official website if you still need help identifying or disputing a charge. Explain what you paid for, what was promised, and what you received. Ask what options may apply to your transaction and whether any time limit is relevant; the evidence supplied for this story does not establish a universal dispute deadline or outcome.

Be cautious of unsolicited messages claiming to be from the FTC, Nuvei, or a “refund service” and demanding an upfront fee or sensitive account details. The FTC site provides a way to report fraud, but the supplied settlement announcement does not direct consumers to a paid claims service or say that consumers must register to receive money. Verify any claim through official channels before sharing information or paying. [S1][S2]

## What to verify before you act

Use this decision path before responding to a charge or a settlement-related message:

- **Can you match the transaction?** Compare its date and amount with receipts, subscriptions, and recent purchases. If the descriptor is unfamiliar, ask your bank or card issuer how to identify it.
- **Do you recognize the merchant and the promise?** Save the offer, invoice, emails, and any messages showing what the merchant said it would provide. Write down what happened while the details are clear.
- **Is the transaction actually tied to Nuvei or to a merchant mentioned in the FTC case?** The supplied announcement does not provide a list of merchants, transaction descriptors, or a way to check an individual payment. Don’t infer a connection from an unfamiliar descriptor alone. [S1]
- **Are you being asked to claim settlement money?** The supplied information gives no consumer claim form, eligibility rules, payment schedule, or deadline. Verify any such instructions through official FTC information rather than links or phone numbers in an unexpected message. [S1][S2]
- **What help do you need now?** For a suspicious charge, contact your payment provider through independently verified contact details and ask what steps are available. For suspected fraud, report it to the FTC. Neither route promises a refund, but both give you a way to raise the concern. [S2]

The practical takeaway is to separate two questions: whether a specific payment deserves follow-up, and whether a consumer can receive money from the Nuvei settlement. The FTC announcement supports the first as a reason to check suspicious transactions; it does not supply enough information to answer the second for any individual consumer. [S1]
