The Federal Trade Commission says payment processor Nuvei will pay $4.85 million and adopt stronger merchant-screening practices to settle charges that it facilitated merchant fraud. The agency alleged that Nuvei and its subsidiaries opened and maintained accounts for merchants they knew or should have known were deceiving consumers, including through tech support scams that took millions of dollars. [S1]

For consumers, the announcement is about how a payment processor handled the businesses using its services—not a notice that consumers are owed a share of the settlement. The supplied FTC announcement describes the payment as a settlement of the agency’s charges; it does not provide a consumer claim form, payout schedule or eligibility rules. Don’t assume the $4.85 million is a refund fund or that you qualify for money based on this announcement alone. [S1]

## What changed

Nuvei and its subsidiaries face a requirement to implement robust merchant-screening practices and a $4.85 million payment under the settlement announced by the FTC on September 4, 2026. The FTC’s complaint alleged that the company processed payments for merchants engaged in deception, including tech support scams. Those are allegations described in the agency’s announcement; the available extract does not spell out the screening procedures Nuvei must use or provide the complete settlement terms. [S1]

That distinction matters. The headline points to a change in the processor’s obligations, but it does not tell an individual consumer whether a particular merchant was involved, whether a specific payment can be reversed, or whether anyone is eligible for compensation. The source describes millions of dollars taken from consumers by tech support scams, but does not identify individual customers or list affected transactions. [S1]

The practical way to read the news is as an enforcement action about oversight of merchants. It is not, on the evidence provided, a finding that every business using Nuvei was fraudulent or that every disputed charge went through Nuvei. Nor does the announcement say consumers should stop using a payment method or that a charge connected to a Nuvei-processed transaction automatically qualifies for relief. [S1]

## What this means for consumers

If you are checking a payment you don’t recognize, start with the transaction itself rather than the settlement headline. Compare the date and amount with your receipts, order confirmations and other records. Try to identify the business you intended to pay, and note any uncertainty about what was sold, what service was promised or whether the payment was authorized. This helps separate a question about a specific merchant from the FTC’s broader allegations about payment processing. [S1]

If the concern is that a seller misled you—for example, you paid for a service that was not delivered as promised—write down what happened and keep the records you already have, such as messages, receipts and payment details. The FTC’s consumer site lists reporting fraud as an available action, but the materials supplied here do not specify what outcome a report will produce or guarantee a refund. [S2]

If you are looking for money back, don’t rely on a message, social post or website claiming that the Nuvei settlement guarantees you a payment. The FTC announcement supplied here gives the settlement amount and describes the agency’s charges; it does not set out a consumer payout process. Verify any claimed program against an official FTC notice before sharing personal or payment information. [S1][S2]

A useful decision path is:

- **You don’t recognize a charge:** collect the transaction details and investigate which business it relates to before drawing a connection to Nuvei.
- **You recognize the seller but dispute the conduct:** keep the relevant records and consider reporting suspected fraud through the FTC’s consumer site. [S2]
- **Someone says you can claim settlement money:** look for an official notice that names the program and explains who can apply; the supplied announcement does not provide those details. [S1]

These steps are ways to organize a concern, not a guarantee of repayment or a legal determination that a business committed fraud. The FTC’s announcement concerns its allegations against Nuvei and the settlement obligations described there. [S1]

## What to verify before you act

Before responding to a settlement-related message or making a claim, verify the details against an official FTC announcement or consumer resource. In particular, check whether there is an actual consumer refund program, who administers it, what transactions qualify, what documentation is required and whether there is a deadline. The supplied material does not answer those questions for this settlement, so treat any specific claim instructions or deadlines as unverified until confirmed through an official source. [S1][S2]

Also verify what your own transaction shows: the business name, date, amount and any receipt or service description. A payment processor settlement does not, by itself, establish that a particular payment was part of the FTC’s allegations. If you suspect fraud, the FTC’s consumer site offers a way to report it; keep expectations measured, because the supplied sources do not promise a particular response or result. [S1][S2]
