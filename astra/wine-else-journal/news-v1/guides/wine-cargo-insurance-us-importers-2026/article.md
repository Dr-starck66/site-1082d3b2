# Wine Cargo Insurance for US Importers: Coverage, Cost Drivers and a 2026 Buying Checklist

*Last editorial review: November 9, 2026. A practical guide for licensed US wine importers, freight buyers and French wineries planning a commercial shipment. This is independent information, not insurance, legal or customs advice.*

A container of wine can travel thousands of miles and still arrive in apparently perfect condition. Yet broken bottles, damaged labels, heat exposure and missing cases can turn a promising purchase order into an expensive dispute. For an American importer sourcing from Languedoc, Roussillon or another French wine region, **wine cargo insurance** deserves the same scrutiny as the producer's price, label documents and shipping quote.

The most useful question is not simply "Is this shipment insured?" It is: **who is insured, for what loss, at which points in the journey, subject to which exclusions, and for how much?**

This guide explains the distinctions that matter, shows how to compare policies without invented premium estimates, and provides a practical insurance request for quotation (RFQ).

## What is wine cargo insurance?

Marine cargo insurance is transit coverage intended to respond to qualifying physical loss or damage to cargo. Despite its name, a policy can cover movements by sea, road and air, depending on its wording. It can be arranged for an individual shipment or under an annual arrangement for recurring imports.

Wine needs a careful reading of coverage terms because its value can be affected by circumstances beyond a shattered bottle. The problems include damaged packaging, leakage, theft, overheating, freezing, interrupted temperature control and concealed damage discovered after delivery.

Coverage is **not universal**. Even a policy marketed as "all risks" has exclusions, conditions, deductibles and claim deadlines. Ask for the actual policy and applicable endorsements; marketing summaries are not enough.

For example, the [Wine and Spirits Shippers Association](https://www.wssa.com/value-protection) describes a program addressing breakage, label damage and temperature events. Those descriptions concern that particular program, not every cargo policy on the market.

## Cargo insurance is not the same as carrier liability

A freight forwarder, trucking company or ocean carrier may owe compensation when it is legally liable for damage, but that is not equivalent to insuring the full commercial value of a wine shipment. Carrier liability can be limited, contested or dependent on proving responsibility.

An importer should therefore ask two separate questions:

1. **Carrier liability:** what can the transport provider be legally required to pay under the applicable contract and law?
2. **Cargo insurance:** what does the purchased policy agree to pay when a covered event occurs?

Do not assume a sentence saying "insured transport" resolves either question. Identify the insurer, named insured or beneficiary, covered route, insured amount and claims procedure.

## Seven coverage questions every wine importer should ask

### 1. Is bottle breakage included?

Check how the policy treats glass breakage, leakage and physical damage during loading, unloading, storage and transshipment. Ask whether an external cause is required and what packaging standards apply.

### 2. Does it cover heat and freezing damage?

This is often the decisive wine-specific question. Wine may deteriorate after excessive temperature exposure without visible damage to the carton. A general cargo policy does **not automatically** cover heat damage, particularly when a dry container was chosen.

Ask the insurer to state in writing whether temperature deterioration is covered for the **actual shipping method**: refrigerated container, insulated equipment or standard dry container. Confirm any required temperature recorders, insulation and alarm thresholds. The [WSSA description of optional temperature coverage](https://www.wssa.com/optional-temperature-coverage) illustrates why an explicit endorsement can matter.

### 3. Are labels, presentation cases and packaging protected?

In the premium wine trade, damaged labels or presentation boxes can make bottles difficult to sell even when their contents remain safe. Check how depreciation, replacement labeling and salvage are assessed. Do not assume that cosmetic deterioration is automatically a covered loss.

### 4. What happens in a theft, shortage or missing-case incident?

Ask whether theft, mysterious disappearance, non-delivery and partial shortage are covered, what evidence is required, and whether sealed-container documentation changes the claims process.

### 5. Where does coverage begin and end?

A door-to-door quotation may start at the French winery, at a consolidator, or only at a port. The end point might be the US port, a bonded warehouse, a distributor or the buyer's own warehouse. Every handoff should appear on the insured itinerary.

### 6. What about delays, strikes and storage?

A delayed container may create a quality and working-capital problem even if it ultimately arrives. **Delay itself is often excluded** from marine cargo insurance. War, strikes, civil unrest, warehouse storage and temperature changes may have separate clauses or exclusions. Ask for the written answer for each risk instead of relying on the phrase "all risks."

### 7. Who can submit the claim and how quickly?

Confirm the legal insured party, loss-payee arrangements, reporting deadlines, survey requirements, document list and deductible. The person who paid freight is not necessarily the person entitled to claim.

## CIF, CIP, FCA and insurance: understand the contract

An Incoterms® rule helps allocate obligations, costs and the transfer of transport risk, but does not replace a sale contract or an insurance policy.

Under **CIF** (Cost, Insurance and Freight), the seller arranges insurance under a maritime term, yet the default insurance obligation is comparatively limited. Under **CIP** (Carriage and Insurance Paid To), Incoterms® 2020 generally require a higher level of insurance protection. The [International Chamber of Commerce](https://iccwbo.org/business-solutions/incoterms-rules/incoterms-2020/) explains the distinct minimum levels of cover.

With terms such as **FCA**, the parties must separately check which participant arranges and pays for cargo insurance. Even under CIF or CIP, the buyer should inspect the certificate and consider additional protection if the supplied coverage is insufficient for wine.

State the complete rule and named place in the commercial contract—for example, the exact French departure point or US destination—and match it to the insurance itinerary. Incoterms do not answer US alcohol licensing questions.

## How much does wine cargo insurance cost in 2026?

There is **no reliable universal premium** for a French-to-US wine shipment. Quoting a fixed rate without a real insurer's offer, declared value and route would be misleading.

The premium and deductible may depend on:

| Pricing factor | What to supply in an RFQ |
| --- | --- |
| Insured value | Currency, invoice value, freight and any agreed uplift |
| Mode and routing | Sea, road, air, consolidation and transshipment points |
| Packaging | Bottles per case, pallets, insulation, temperature equipment |
| Commodity | Wine type, bottle size, total volume, declared sensitivity |
| Season and duration | Planned shipping dates, dwell time and likely heat exposure |
| Scope of cover | Breakage, theft, temperature variation, warehouse extensions |
| Claims history | Relevant previous losses, if requested by the insurer |
| Deductible and limits | Requested retention, policy limit and special sublimits |

For a clean comparison, ask at least two qualified brokers or insurers to quote the **same insured value, route, packaging and coverage requirements**. A cheaper premium with a temperature exclusion is not necessarily a better quotation for wine.

### Example: compare two quotes without misleading arithmetic

Imagine that a buyer has received Quote A and Quote B for the same shipment. Quote A is cheaper, but excludes temperature damage in a dry container. Quote B costs more and includes a documented heat-damage extension subject to insulation and temperature-recording conditions.

There is no rational choice until the buyer has checked the expected route, shipment value, deductible, claims wording and whether the proposed packing method satisfies the conditions. This is a **hypothetical decision example**, not a quoted insurance premium or an estimate of loss probability.

## The insurance RFQ checklist: copy into your broker email

Send a structured brief. The exact requirements will differ, but the following are a useful starting point:

- Importer and exporter legal names; insured party and beneficiary
- Winery collection address, ports, transshipment points and final US delivery address
- Pickup and estimated delivery dates; anticipated storage periods
- Commercial invoice value, currency, freight and requested insured amount
- Number of cases, bottles, pallet count, gross weight and packaging specification
- Incoterms® 2020 rule and the exact named place
- Transport mode, container type and any temperature-control equipment
- Requested cover for breakage, labels, theft, shortage and concealed damage
- Requested cover for overheating, freezing, reefer failure and power interruption
- War/strikes requirements, exclusions, deductibles and maximum limits
- Inspection, notice-of-loss and claim-document deadlines
- Whether the quote is one-off or part of an annual open policy

Ask the broker to provide the **full wording, exclusions, endorsements, sum insured, premium, deductible, claims contacts and coverage start/end points** before paying.

## A cargo insurance policy does not replace a wine importer permit

The insurance decision sits alongside—not in place of—federal and state alcohol compliance.

For US commercial imports, the [Alcohol and Tobacco Tax and Trade Bureau (TTB)](https://www.ttb.gov/import-export/itd/importing-bottled-alcohol-beverages-into-the-united-states/) explains the Federal Basic Importer's Permit and label-approval requirements where applicable. The TTB states that it does **not charge a fee to issue the federal basic importer permit**. That does not mean shipping, licensing at other levels or operating an import business is free.

The [US Customs and Border Protection guidance](https://www.help.cbp.gov/s/article/Article1076?language=en_US) addresses commercial alcohol import requirements and applicable state restrictions. The [FDA's food-import guidance](https://www.fda.gov/food/food-imports-exports/importing-food-products-united-states) explains facility registration and prior-notice requirements where they apply.

Before dispatch, have the licensed importer, customs broker and appropriate advisers verify the current permit, COLA, import, labeling, duties, taxes and state-level obligations for the exact wine and destination. Buying insurance does not authorize an otherwise non-compliant shipment.

For the broader import sequence, see WINE ELSE's [TTB permit and COLA checklist](https://else.wine/blog/guides/import-french-wine-usa-ttb-permit-cola-checklist/).

## Preventing a claim is as important as buying the policy

Start risk control at the winery. Agree on pallet configuration, carton quality, wrapping, loading photographs and the handoff record. For temperature-sensitive shipments, discuss seasonal departure windows, insulating materials or reefer equipment with the freight provider.

At collection and receipt, document seal numbers, visible damage, missing cartons, temperature logs and reservations on transport documents. Keep invoices, packing lists, bills of lading, delivery evidence and correspondence organized so a claims handler can understand what happened.

A policy may cover some losses and exclude others. Good preventive documentation helps the parties manage either result.

## Questions US importers ask most often

### Do all marine cargo policies cover wine damaged by heat?

No. Temperature deterioration requires careful wording and may need a specific endorsement, type of container, insulation or evidence of an insured event. Obtain written confirmation for the intended shipment.

### Is cargo insurance mandatory to import wine into the USA?

Do not confuse a commercial or lender requirement to insure a shipment with the legal authorization to import alcoholic beverages. Federal and state import requirements must be checked separately. Whether insurance is contractually required depends on the transaction and its terms.

### Does an "all-risk" policy cover every possible loss?

No. It remains subject to exclusions, warranties, conditions, deductibles and applicable limits. Ask for the complete policy wording.

### Is the cheapest quote the best option?

Not without comparing the scope of cover. A lower premium can come with narrower temperature protection, higher deductibles or different claim conditions.

### Can a French winery arrange insurance for an American importer?

Sometimes, depending on the sale contract, Incoterms rule and insurer. Both parties should document who procures coverage, who is insured, and who can receive claim payments.

The useful process is: select the wine, clarify the importer and distribution route, confirm the legal documentation, calculate landed cost, specify shipping controls and then compare insurance terms against the real risks of that route.
