Bordeaux Wine Exports Fall in Value: Why US Buyers Should Read Beyond the Headline

Regional customs figures show Bordeaux wine export volumes and values down over the year to July 2026. Here is how to interpret the figures without overstating the US opportunity.

By WINE ELSE Editorial Desk · Published 1 November 2026 · Sources independently identified below

A market signal in the customs statistics

According to DRAAF Nouvelle-Aquitaine’s September market report, using French customs figures to the end of July 2026, Bordeaux wine exports on a rolling twelve-month basis exceeded 1.31 million hectolitres and reached around €1.82 billion. Compared with the corresponding previous period, that represents a fall of 7% in volume and 14% in value. The windows matter: these are cumulative twelve-month comparisons, not the result for July alone.

What the United States figures actually show

The same regional report lists the United States at about 196,000 hectolitres of Bordeaux wine exports on the reported period, ahead of China at about 109,000 hectolitres. Yet shipments to the US were also lower, by approximately 10% in volume. The report also records a sharper decline in US export value. A high ranking among destinations can coexist with a shrinking market.

Do lower values equal lower winery prices?

Not necessarily. Customs export values aggregate many categories and channels. Shifts in product mix, sales destination and price segment can change total values independently of any individual producer’s ex-cellar quote. A distributor cannot infer an offer price for a specific Bordeaux label from the regional totals alone. Likewise, weaker exports do not automatically mean there is surplus stock in every appellation.

The broader trade context

DRAAF points to softer global wine consumption and geopolitical uncertainty as a difficult background for international trade. It also discusses vineyard removals designed to rebalance supply and demand. Those forces affect different areas and business models in different ways, so the observed aggregate export trend should not be projected onto all French wine.

A disciplined approach for importers

A more useful purchasing framework compares the same currency, vintage, format and Incoterm across producer proposals. Ask whether the allocation is secured, whether a US importer is already appointed and what documentation or approval is needed before entry into the US market. Freight, insurance, taxes and domestic distribution margins deserve their own line items; none are answered by headline export statistics.
