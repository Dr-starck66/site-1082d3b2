TTB Wine Importer Basic Permit: Application Preparation for US Buyers

Prepare for a TTB federal wine importer basic permit with a legal-entity checklist, responsible roles and practical questions before shipping French wine.

Published November 5, 2026 · WINE ELSE editorial · Regulatory information, not legal advice

At a glance: A viable commercial shipment needs a named responsible importer, product-specific approvals where required, an authorized sales route and a documented total cost. The checklist below explains how to verify the relevant steps.

Who should apply for the import permit?

The TTB importer application is for the US business that will commercially import beverage alcohol. It is not a permit that a French winery can simply lend to an American customer, and it is not interchangeable with a customs broker’s services. Determine the US entity that will hold the permit and take legal responsibility before submitting any shipment instructions. A producer selling ex-cellar wine should request the importer’s legal details rather than relying solely on a trade name or website.

Information to assemble before filing

Create an application folder with the entity’s legal name and registration details, business premises, ownership and authorized-signatory information, contact details, proposed activities and any required supporting forms. TTB’s online packet identifies forms and documents for the importer category, including the Basic Permit application. Different business structures and circumstances can require different attachments; use the current official checklist rather than copying a generic example from another importer. Retain dated evidence of submissions and regulator correspondence.

Importer and wholesaler are not the same permit category

A business can import wine for its authorized operations, but taking on wholesale distribution adds another regulatory question. TTB’s guidance says businesses intending to act both as importer and wholesaler should apply for the applicable permits. State regulation may restrict which entity sells to which tier or where the wine can move. Clarifying those responsibilities at the outset prevents a French producer from relying on a partner who has shipping contacts but no lawful route to market.

Do not confuse business approval with product clearance

Even an approved importer permit does not establish that an individual wine is ready for release. The brand, label, alcohol content, bottle format, FDA-related requirements and customs entry remain important. Imported wines in covered categories normally require a valid COLA before release from Customs custody. Ask the importer to show how each selected SKU and label version will be handled. Never reuse a label approval blindly across materially different products or designs.

A useful permit-status request for a buyer

Ask for the full company identity, permit-holder name, business role, confirmation that the relevant importer authorization is active, the US state in which commercial distribution is planned, and the person responsible for label approvals. Do not demand private authentication details or sensitive personal documents by ordinary email. A qualified importer should be able to explain who checks permits and what documentary evidence can lawfully be shared with counterparties.

Plan the commercial timeline around evidence, not estimates

TTB applications involve review, and processing time depends on circumstances. Do not guarantee an approval date or promise a shipment based on a draft filing. Build an initial sales plan with contingent milestones: importer approved, labels reviewed, FDA and customs obligations established, pricing agreed and only then freight booked. Record which milestone is verified and which is still pending. This makes a winery–importer negotiation easier to audit and reduces surprise costs.

Frequently asked question

Does a TTB import permit automatically authorize wholesale distribution?

No. TTB states that an importer who also plans to wholesale alcohol beverages must address the relevant wholesaler permit requirements; state permissions are separate.
