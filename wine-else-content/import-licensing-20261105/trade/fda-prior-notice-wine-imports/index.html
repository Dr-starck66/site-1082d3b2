FDA Prior Notice for Wine Imports: Registration and Shipment Checklist

What French wine exporters and US importers should verify about FDA facility registration, prior notice, shipment data and customs coordination.

Published November 5, 2026 · WINE ELSE editorial · Regulatory information, not legal advice

At a glance: A viable commercial shipment needs a named responsible importer, product-specific approvals where required, an authorized sales route and a documented total cost. The checklist below explains how to verify the relevant steps.

Why FDA matters even when the product is alcohol

Wine is a beverage and can fall within US food-import requirements in addition to the TTB alcohol regime. FDA explains that facilities involved in producing, processing, packing or holding food may need registration and that prior notice is generally required for covered imported-food shipments. The relevant requirements depend on the facility and transaction, and exemptions exist. Treat statements like “wine is regulated only by TTB” as a reason to pause and verify rather than proceed.

Identify facilities and roles, not just the shipper

Prepare an accurate map of the French producer, any relevant storage or packing facility, the US importer, consignee, customs broker and shipment route. Clarify who confirms any required FDA facility registration and who files the prior notice. The party handling the commercial purchase is not necessarily the party submitting electronic customs and food-import data. Written assignments reduce last-minute uncertainty at the port.

What a prior-notice record must relate to

The filing should be prepared from the actual shipment information rather than an early marketing spreadsheet. Match the product description, responsible facility, quantity, packaging, country of origin, transport details and arrival information as required by the applicable filing system. FDA guidance explains that notices can be submitted through authorized electronic channels and must be received within the applicable time window. Requirements differ by mode and circumstance, so confirm the current procedure for each shipment with the responsible filer.

Samples are not automatically exempt from documentation

Small commercial samples, quality-control bottles and products used for market research should not be assumed exempt simply because they are not yet offered for retail sale. FDA guidance addresses imported-food samples and specific exceptions. The existence of a carrier’s sample service does not replace the importer's obligations. Before requesting sample bottles from a French winery, agree on the shipment purpose, legal importer, documentation and permitted route.

Check the timing close to the actual shipping date

FDA issued amendments affecting prior notice of goods arriving by international mail, with implementation details and enforcement expectations that can change. Instead of reproducing one fixed universal deadline in a marketing article, verify the current regulation and mode-specific filing instructions on the FDA’s official page when booking. If a shipment date or route changes, have the responsible party assess whether the filing needs correction or resubmission. Keep confirmation identifiers in the shipment record.

Create one compliance packet for each purchase order

Keep the importer’s legal information, winery technical sheet, product and label records, applicable FDA registration confirmation, prior-notice acknowledgement, broker details, commercial invoice, packing list, customs information and responsible contacts together. Log the source and last verification date for regulatory statements. This packet does not prove approval by itself, but it makes professional review faster and helps an independent Occitanie estate answer a qualified US buyer consistently.

Frequently asked question

Does TTB approval replace FDA prior notice for wine?

No. Alcohol import licensing and FDA food-import requirements are distinct regulatory workstreams. Confirm the applicable FDA requirements and exemptions for the actual shipment.
