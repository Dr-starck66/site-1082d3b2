Imported French Wine COLA: US Label Approval Checklist Before Shipping

How US importers and French wineries coordinate imported wine COLA, label details, approval records and shipment-ready artwork without costly relabeling surprises.

Published November 5, 2026 · WINE ELSE editorial · Regulatory information, not legal advice

At a glance: A viable commercial shipment needs a named responsible importer, product-specific approvals where required, an authorized sales route and a documented total cost. The checklist below explains how to verify the relevant steps.

Why label approval belongs early in the sourcing process

US label rules should be reviewed before a French producer prints thousands of bottles for an American order. An approved importer may need a TTB Certificate of Label Approval, commonly called a COLA, for the imported wine. The artwork that looks correct in France may still need additional or differently presented US information. Coordinating label development early is a commercial decision: the cost of changing a digital design is far smaller than rebuilding stock after a shipment has arrived.

Start with one precisely described wine SKU

Prepare a document for each proposed product: producer and brand, appellation or origin, wine type, vintage where shown, alcohol content, bottle volume, front and back label artwork, and all required disclosures. Confirm that the product description and the label accurately reflect the wine. The permitted terminology and disclosure requirements vary by product and governing rule. Never create a fictional certification, award, importer address or government approval to make a mockup appear finished.

Assign responsibility to the licensed importing business

TTB describes the imported-wine COLA process as involving the importer. A French estate can provide technical specifications, artwork and certifications where applicable, but the US importer must direct the US regulatory review and submission process. Ask which legal entity is filing, whether the review concerns the exact SKU and whether a written approval or other applicable route is available. The existence of a draft label in a shared folder is not a COLA.

Distinguish approval, exemption and market access

Different beverages and circumstances can be subject to different labeling regimes and exceptions. Avoid statements that every bottle always follows precisely the same approval path. Verify the product’s classification and whether the contemplated sale and shipment require a COLA or another authorized route. Label approval is also not a substitute for state distribution licensing, FDA obligations or customs clearance. Keep these approvals as separate entries in the shipment dossier.

Avoid common errors before ordering packaging

Check the consistency of producer names and addresses, wine origin statements, alcohol content, mandatory warning information and any importer information applicable to the product. Compare final printed artwork against the submitted and approved version. Confirm legibility and that marketing claims can be supported. Ask a responsible specialist to review unfamiliar terms, unusual formats and blend-related statements. Preserve the final label PDF and the corresponding approval or compliance record with the vintage and shipment reference.

A practical handoff between Occitanie and the US

The producer sends the final technical sheet and editable artwork. The importer confirms legal classification and review route, requests adjustments in writing, and records the approved version before bottling or release. Procurement then checks that purchase order, invoice, packing list and label refer to the same SKU. WINE ELSE’s role is to facilitate well-specified trade conversations, not to certify that a label is legally approved.

Frequently asked question

Can a French-language wine label be shipped to the US without review?

Do not assume so. The responsible importer should assess the exact wine and full label against applicable US requirements and obtain approval or an applicable authorization before release.
